The short version

How IPT rankings work, in 30 seconds

  • At every tournament, players earn ranking points depending on how far they get. Winning earns the most.
  • Bigger events pay more: winning a Grand Slam is worth points, winning a City Open .
  • Points don't last forever. After a while they drop off, and your ranking falls unless you win new ones.
  • Your ranking is simply the total of the points you still have.

Why two dates?

The IPT doesn't publish exactly when points expire. Player pages say points count for "12 months", but points won in August and September 2025 still count today, more than 12 months later. The data behaves as if a whole season's points drop off together on 1 July.

End of 2026 shows what happens if the 12-month rule is applied. 1 July 2027 shows what happens if last season's points all drop off at the start of next season. The real answer is probably one of these two.

This season pays less

The same result earns fewer points this season (2026-27) than last season. So even a player who repeats every result from last year will end up with fewer points.

Questions

Where does the data come from?

Everything comes from the public Indian Padel Tour website (indianpadeltour.in): rankings, every player's tournament history, and full results for the three tournaments the site still shows (Mumbai City Open 5.0, Goa Grand Slam 11.0 and Kochi City Open 2.0). It was collected slowly, one page at a time.

Why can't a player keep their points by repeating last year's results?

Because this season's points scale is lower. Winning a City Open used to be worth 400 points and is now worth 300, so the same finish replaces fewer points than it loses.

What are the limits?
  • The expiry rule for points isn't published, so two possible rules are shown.
  • "To stay at the same rank" assumes nobody else wins points, so real targets are higher.
  • Only three finished tournaments have match-level results; ranking results go back to December 2024.
Is this official?

No. It's an independent analysis of public data and isn't connected to the Indian Padel Tour.
